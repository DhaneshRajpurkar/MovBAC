How to read this
  • Hotspot = one satellite pixel where a sensor saw unusually hot ground, usually a fire.
  • MODIS (1 km pixels, since 2000) is being retired. VIIRS (375 m pixels, since 2012) sees smaller fires, so it reports more hotspots for the same fires.
  • Raw view switches sensors in 2012, so you'll see a fake "jump" that has nothing to do with real fires.
  • Harmonized converts the old MODIS counts into VIIRS-equivalent counts, so the whole 2001→today record is on one scale.
  • Anomaly shows each month compared with the median of the same month in all years. Orange is busier than normal and purple is quieter.

Map

Dots show hotspots in the selected date range, colored by Fire Radiative Power (FRP = how much heat the fire gives off, in megawatts). Click a dot for details. The 3D button under + / − tilts the map over the spot you are looking at: zoomed out you get terrain with a fire heat map draped over it, zoomed in to a city you get 3D buildings (photorealistic Google 3D when it is set up), with the fire dots placed on them. When there are more than 5,000 detections, the map shows colored squares instead: each square counts every detection inside it (darker red = more), and you can click a square for its numbers.

Yearly totals: why harmonization matters

Raw MODIS and raw VIIRS disagree for the same fires (VIIRS sees about 3–5× more). The thick harmonized line uses VIIRS where we have it and scaled-up MODIS everywhere else, so all years and places are on one scale.
